# Text-first, no-photo & fast sites

Recipe sites your phone can render in two seconds on a two-bar connection. Mostly plain text and tables; nothing autoplaying, nothing tracking you across the web. Blurb note: **two lines each — what it is, why you'd flip to it tonight.**

## The picks

### [The Wikibooks Cookbook](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook "Main page of the open-edit Cookbook on Wikibooks")
The open-licensed recipe wiki: plain text recipes and technique explainers with no ads and no script load at all.
Tonight use: when you need the *method* ("what does deglaze do?") more than a loving story — it loads before your noodle water boils.

### [Recipeland](https://www.recipeland.com/ "Recipeland — classic text recipe archive")
A search-first archive of tens of thousands of short, text-style recipes, straight out of the web's useful bake sale era.
Tonight use: type an ingredient, get modest exact recipes without a single hero-image scroll or recipe-card pop-up.

### [Cooking for Engineers](https://www.cookingforengineers.com/ "Cooking for Engineers")
Recipes and technique explainers written in clean prose, tables, and diagrams by people who care about the why.
Tonight use: when you want to finally understand *why* the pan or the heat matters — the "recipe table" format is a genuine invention.

---

## Why no-photo matters tonight

- Photos cost bandwidth: a photography-heavy recipe page can be 20× the size of its text. On a capped plan or a weak signal, that's the difference between cooking and giving up.
- Text-first pages also tend to be older and calmer — no floating "subscribe!" banners, no autoplay video, no 14 select-agreement cookies.

When you just want dinner with zero web traffic at all, that's the [Tonight page](tonight.html).
