# Library & free-culture archives

These are the deep wells: public-domain cookbooks you can read free and legally online, backed by real libraries and open collections. Blurb note: **two lines each — what it is, why you'd flip to it tonight.**

## The picks

### [Cookbooks and Cooking — Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/bookshelf/419 "[Cookbooks and Cooking] A bookshelf from Project Gutenberg")
Hundreds of full-text out-of-copyright cookbooks: *The White House Cookbook*, *Mrs Beeton*-era classics, regional community cookbooks.
Tonight use: search the shelf for your ingredient, and a 100-year-old version of the recipe shows up with the extra confidence of a recipe that survived a century.

### [Cookbooks — Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/subjects/cookbooks "Open Library subject page for cookbooks")
Way more cookbooks than a good public library wall, all readable in-browser (older = fully opened, free).
Tonight use: instant, no-login look at any vintage cookbook when you want to check how a dish is *supposed* to come together before you improvise.

### [Cookbooks — Internet Archive](https://archive.org/search?query=cookbooks&and%5B%5D=mediatype%3A%22texts%22 "Internet Archive full-text search for cookbooks")
Full-text search inside thousands of scanned cookbooks — church cookbooks, brand test-kitchen booklets, 1970s casserole gold.
Tonight use: the "search inside the book" is an ingredient-specific treasure map that no recipe-author-influencer site can match.

### [The Wikibooks Cookbook](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook "Main page of the open-edit Cookbook on Wikibooks")
A genuinely collaborative, ad-free, open-licensed recipe wiki — plain text-then-prose style, no pop-ups, no "my journey with beans".
Tonight use: clean versions of basic techniques (how a roux is built, what braising is) when you want to understand instead of scroll.

### [What's on the Menu? — NYPL](https://menus.nypl.org/ "New York Public Library's historic menu collection")
Deeply fun crowdsourced archive of transcribed restaurant menus, 1850s–-present from the New York Public Library.
Tonight use: not for instructions — for *ideas*. Random-browse the 1880s and steal a dish name that fits what you already have.
